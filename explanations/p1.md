# Phase 1 — FSK in simulation

## Objectives

The objectives in this phase is basically to understand how a basic modulation works, in this case, FSK, where we modulate the information (bits) with diferent frequencies. This will be the basis for the next phases, where
we will use this FSK to transmit real data, in a real channel. 

## Implementation

Here I made two basic functions, modulation and demodulation, in modulation the function takes some chain of bits and modulate them, if the bit is a 1 we generate a signal with the frequency assigned to bit 1, this signal has a determined length, given by the bit rate. Then at the demodulation we just cut the received signal into pieces of the length of a symbol and determine which is the symbol sent. We do this using a incoherent demodulator, meaning that we don't need to know the phase of the signal. This is because in FSK we modulate using frequency, not phase, as other modulations that I will use later on will be. Basically, we just have to look which frequency has more energy, the one associated with bit 0 or bit 1, and assigned it given this idea.

## Results

I tested the modulation by giving a simple 3-bit vector, (101) and seeing if it was modulating it correctly, and yes it was, we can see in time that the cosine in the middle has a different frequency than those at the edges, and in frequency, the frequency assigned for the bit 1 (9000 Hz) has a bigger energy that the one assigned to bit (0).
Then I tested the demodulation code, just seeing if there was no errors in demodulation (with no noise) and there was not, also,testing how the BER evolves in relation to the Eb/N0 (energy bit per Noise, the equivalent to SNR for digital communications).
